## Q1. Are the mathematical foundations and model descriptions in notes 01, 03, 04, 08, 09, 13 and 17 accurate and current?

### Takeaway
The mathematical core of note 01 (independent Poisson, Maher attack/defence, the Dixon–Coles low-score correction, exponential time decay, identifiability, no random split) is correct and still the industry reference point. Two descriptions are only **partially correct**. First, "negative binomial for overdispersion": conditional on team strength, goals are close to equidispersed or even underdispersed, and NB2 cannot represent underdispersion. Second, "bivariate Poisson is better for draws": the gain comes mainly from **diagonal inflation**, and plain bivariate Poisson allows only non-negative correlation. The roadmap in notes 03/08 omits the rating families that won recent benchmark competitions (pi-ratings, GBT on ratings) and player-rating models, which have the best recent evidence of beating bookmakers.

### Inferences
- In its own words, the knowledge base is correct but its information set is thin. Every implemented goal model uses only past scores, while the literature consistently finds that odds contain more information than results ([Wunderlich & Memmert 2018](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0198668); [Hvattum & Arntzen 2010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijforecast.2009.10.002); [Forrest et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijforecast.2005.03.003)). The measured result is exactly what the literature predicts: log-loss about 1.019–1.025 against about 0.996 for the market in all 22 leagues, with ECE close to the market. Changing the model structure (Poisson → NB2 → GAS) moves log-loss by about 0.01 at most. Closing the gap of about 0.023 nats needs new information such as lineups/players, xG or shot quality, which is what note 08 calls v1–v2.
- Note 01 should add a line saying that NB2 cannot represent underdispersion and that its dispersion parameter α will collapse to the lower bound when goals are near-Poisson. The better candidates for goals are the Weibull count model, a Sarmanov-type DC extension or a diagonal-inflated bivariate Poisson. NB2 is better suited to corners and cards.
- The sign convention for defence differs between note 01 and the code. It is harmless, but it should be documented to prevent sign errors in future features.

### Gaps
- I could not access the full text of DC 1997 in this session. The τ formula used here, τ(0,0) = 1 − λμρ, τ(0,1) = 1 + λρ, τ(1,0) = 1 + μρ, τ(1,1) = 1 − ρ with λ the home mean and μ the away mean, is the standard textbook form. The DC ξ value was confirmed through a secondary source (Boshnakov et al.).
- I did not verify claims about referees, weather, H2H and congestion (notes 04 and 17) against primary studies. They are sensible design hypotheses but not literature-backed facts in these notes.

## Q3. Do the implementations match their textbook definitions (DC τ and time decay, NB2, GAS update, Davidson draw parameter)?

### Takeaway
The formulas are implemented correctly: the DC τ, exponential weights, the NB2 pmf, the GAS Poisson score with unit scaling and the Davidson probabilities. The main deviations are in **how the models are used**, and they bias the internal model comparison.
- **DC and NB2 are fitted once per outer season and frozen for the whole season.** DC 1997 re-estimates before every prediction date.
- **NB2 has no time decay at all.**
- The nested plan offers DC **only one half-life (365 days)**.
- Promoted teams get league-average strength ("neutral") for a whole season.
- GAS uses fixed step and persistence values from a 3-point grid instead of ML estimation, and it pulls every team toward the league mean instead of toward a team-specific level.

These deviations likely explain much of GAS/Elo's 0.014 log-loss "win" over DC/NB2. They do not explain the gap to the market.

### Cited Findings

**Dixon–Coles (`app/src/model_buk/models/dixon_coles.py`)**
- τ matches DC 1997 (with λ the home rate and μ the away rate): τ(0,0) = 1 − λμρ, τ(0,1) = 1 + λρ, τ(1,0) = 1 + μρ, τ(1,1) = 1 − ρ (lines 196–207 and `low_score_correction`). The code also rejects τ ≤ 0. **Matches the textbook form.** — [Dixon & Coles 1997](https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-9876.00065); DC as "moving probabilities between the scores 0-0, 0-1, 1-0, and 1-1": [Michels et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1093/jrsssc/qlae050)
- ρ is box-bounded to [−0.3, 0.3] (line 241) instead of DC's λ/μ-dependent admissible range. Because τ > 0 is also enforced, this is a practical, conservative restriction. **Acceptable.** — [DC 1997](https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-9876.00065)
- Weights are `exp(−ξ·age_days)`, with the reference date set to the fold start (line 98 and `goals_backtest.py:85-90`). Weighting the whole log-likelihood, including log τ, is DC's pseudo-likelihood. **Matches.** DC used half-weeks with ξ = 0.0065, about a 373-day half-life ([Boshnakov et al. 2017 AAM](https://pure.manchester.ac.uk/ws/files/49399144/ijfpaper.pdf)).
- **Deviation 1 (important):** `walk_forward_dixon_coles` fits once per fold (`goals_backtest.py:76-90`), and the folds are whole seasons from 1 July to 1 July (`app/config/nested_goals_plan.example.json`). DC 1997 and later users fit at time t "using all matches played up to, but not including, time t" ([Boshnakov et al. 2017 AAM](https://pure.manchester.ac.uk/ws/files/49399144/ijfpaper.pdf), reproducing the DC pseudo-likelihood). GAS and Elo update after every matchday in the test season (`goals_backtest.py:145-200`), so the DC/NB2 vs GAS/Elo comparison is not like-for-like.
- **Deviation 2:** the nested plan has only one DC candidate (`half_life_days: 365`, `nested_goals_plan.example.json:4-10`). A grid function exists (`goals_backtest.py:457-466`), but the nested selection does not tune ξ, even though note 01 says to "wyznaczyć poza próbą" (determine it out of sample).
- **Deviation 3:** unseen (promoted) teams get attack = defence = 0, i.e. league average (`unknown_strategy="neutral"`, `goals_backtest.py:96`). With season-long freezing, promoted teams stay average for the whole season. This is my inference from the code; I found no paper that prescribes a specific promoted-team prior.
- Non-textbook additions: an L2 ridge penalty on team effects, and an `xg_weight` pseudo-likelihood term that plugs non-integer xG into a Poisson log-pmf through `gammaln`. Both are reasonable regularisers but not part of DC 1997. The xG term is a quasi-likelihood. It is disabled (`xg_weight: 0`) in the nested plan.

**NB2 (`negative_binomial.py`)**
- The pmf uses size = 1/α and p = size/(size + μ), giving Var = μ + αμ². **This is the textbook NB2.** — [Cameron & Trivedi 2013](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9781139013567)
- The log-likelihood is written stably near the Poisson boundary, α is bounded in [1e-8, 1e4], and the margins are independent (no DC τ).
- **Deviation 4 (important):** there is no recency weighting at all ("this static model intentionally applies no recency weighting", in the docstring), plus season-long freezing. Every training season counts equally, which contradicts the dynamic-strength literature ([Koopman & Lit 2015](https://doi.org/10.1111/rssa.12042); [Ley et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1177/1471082X18817650)).
- **Structural limitation:** NB2 cannot capture underdispersion, and home goals look slightly underdispersed (c_H = 1.050 in [Boshnakov et al. 2017](https://pure.manchester.ac.uk/ws/files/49399144/ijfpaper.pdf)). α is therefore expected to sit near the lower bound, making NB2 almost Poisson without the DC correction.

**GAS-Poisson (`gas_poisson.py`)**
- The update is `att_H ← B·att_H + A·(y_H − λ_H)` and `def_A ← B·def_A + A·(y_H − λ_H)` (lines 74–89). For log λ_H = c + HA + att_H + def_A, the derivative ∂log p/∂att_H = ∂log p/∂def_A = y_H − λ_H. **The score is correct.** Unscaled (unit-matrix) scores are also what Koopman & Lit used: "we set the scaling to the unity matrix, that is S = I₄". Their update is `f_{ij,t+1} = ω_ij + B_ij f_ijt + A_ij s_ijt`. — [Koopman & Lit 2019, VU repository PDF](https://research.vu.nl/ws/portalfiles/portal/151597369/Forecasting_football_match_results_in_national_league_competitions_using_scoredriven_time_series_models.pdf); GAS framework: [Creal, Koopman & Lucas 2013, J. Appl. Econometrics 28(5):777–795](https://doi.org/10.1002/jae.1279)
- **Deviation 5:** Koopman & Lit's static parameters (A, B, λ3, δ) "are estimated by maximum likelihood". The repo instead chooses A ∈ {0.02, 0.04, 0.08} and B ∈ {0.99, 0.98, …} from a grid, and fixes the intercept and home advantage at the training-sample goal means (lines 127–128) without re-estimating them. — [Koopman & Lit 2019](https://research.vu.nl/ws/portalfiles/portal/151597369/Forecasting_football_match_results_in_national_league_competitions_using_scoredriven_time_series_models.pdf)
- **Deviation 6:** in Koopman & Lit, ω is team-specific (ω_ij = M_ij ω), so each team's strength reverts to its own long-run level. The repo uses ω = 0 and recentres after each match, so every team reverts to the league average. With B = 0.98 per team match, about 0.98^38 ≈ 0.46 of a state survives one 38-match season (my calculation). This is strong shrinkage toward the mean.
- **Deviation 7:** the repo uses independent Poisson margins. Koopman & Lit found the bivariate Poisson (pairwise counts) "delivers the most precise forecasts" and the Skellam (goal difference) specification the best betting results ([Koopman & Lit 2019 abstract](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijforecast.2018.10.011)).

**Elo–Davidson (`elo_davidson.py`) and `backtest/elo.py`**
- Probabilities are P(H) ∝ π_H, P(D) ∝ ν√(π_H π_A), P(A) ∝ π_A with π = 10^{r/400} (line 54). **This is Davidson's (1970) tie extension of Bradley–Terry.** — [Davidson 1970, JASA 65(329):317–328](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1970.10481082)
- Draw parameter: ν̂ = 2d/(1−d) (line 93). This inverts P(D) = ν/(2+ν) for equal teams. **Algebraically correct**, but it plugs in the pooled draw rate of all matches, most of which are between unequal teams, so it slightly underestimates ν (my inference).
- The update target is E = P(H) + ½P(D) (line 75). This matches the "Elo-Davidson" algorithm. Szczecinski & Djebbi show classical Elo is a special case of it, and that standard Elo "silently assumes games with 50% of draws". — [Szczecinski & Djebbi 2020, JQAS 16(3):211–220](https://doi.org/10.1515/jqas-2019-0102)
- Not implemented, though standard in football Elo: a goal-difference multiplier on K ([Hvattum & Arntzen 2010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijforecast.2009.10.002)), the score-margin-based pi-ratings ([Constantinou & Fenton 2013](https://doi.org/10.1515/jqas-2012-0036)) and between-season regression to the mean.
- `backtest/elo.py` pairs standard Elo (K = 20, HA = 60) with a multinomial logit on the Elo difference (`backtest/walkforward.py`). This is methodologically close to Hvattum & Arntzen's ordered-logit approach. **Consistent with the textbook approach.**

**Market residual (`market_residual.py`)**
- A multinomial logit with de-vigged opening log-odds as a fixed offset and bounded, L2-penalised corrections. Imputation and standardisation are fitted on the training data only. This design is **consistent** with the odds-plus-data literature ([Egidi et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1177/1471082X18798414); [Wunderlich & Memmert 2018](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0198668)). The "opening" price is the football-data.co.uk Friday snapshot, not a true market opening, as the project itself documents in `HANDOFF.md` ("czym jest piątkowe zdjęcie", i.e. what the Friday snapshot is).

**Corner model (`corner_dual_v02.py`)**
- Two CatBoost regressors on corner counts. `fit_dual_model` passes through whatever `params` the config supplies, and I did not check `app/config/corners_v02.toml`. Unless the loss is set to a count objective, CatBoost's default is RMSE, so the model gives conditional means and O/U-corner probabilities need an assumed count distribution. This is my inference; I did not check how corner probabilities are derived downstream.

### Inferences
- The internal ranking "GAS/Elo > DC/NB2 in 19–20 of 22 leagues, about 0.014 log-loss" is confounded by refit cadence. GAS and Elo learn during the test season, while DC and NB2 are frozen from 1 July and treat promoted teams as average. A fair re-test would refit DC weekly (or per matchday) with a tuned ξ grid of about 180–1,200-day half-lives, spanning the published values, and use a promoted-team prior. The expected effect is a smaller gap to GAS. The distance to the market (about 0.02 nats) will probably not close, because the information set is unchanged.
- The GAS implementation is "Koopman–Lit-inspired" rather than Koopman–Lit. Estimating (A, B, team-specific ω, HA) by ML and adding a bivariate-Poisson or DC τ term would make it textbook-conformant.

### Gaps
- I did not verify the exact DC 1997 feasible range of ρ or the equation numbers from the full text. They come from the standard form of the model.
- I did not inspect how the corner model's means are turned into threshold probabilities.

## Q4. Which well-established model families are missing, and would any plausibly add information beyond market odds?

### Takeaway
Most missing *structural* models (bivariate or diagonal-inflated Poisson, Skellam, Weibull count, copulas, the Sarmanov DC extension, hierarchical Bayes) would mostly reshape the score distribution. That matters for draws, O/U and BTTS, but adds little **information** beyond odds. The families with published evidence of information beyond bookmaker prices are the **player/lineup rating models** (Holmes & McHale 2024; Arntzen & Hvattum 2021) and, more weakly, **xG or shot-quality ratings** (Wheatcroft's GAP ratings; Wilkens 2026). The best off-the-shelf "results-only" benchmark missing from the repo is **pi-ratings plus gradient-boosted trees**.

## Q5. Is the evaluation methodology consistent with best practice?

### Takeaway
Largely yes, and it is ahead of much of the published football literature: nested walk-forward, log-loss as the primary metric, Brier and RPS as secondary metrics, the de-vigged closing market as the benchmark, a date-clustered bootstrap and per-league gates. Four things need updating:
1. The code's claim that RPS is "the proper metric for 1X2" is contested (Wheatcroft 2021 recommends the log/ignorance score).
2. ECE with 20 equal-width bins pooled across H/D/A is unstable. Use CORP reliability diagrams and the miscalibration/discrimination decomposition.
3. Add paired forecast-comparison tests (Diebold–Mariano) and correct for multiple comparisons across 22 leagues.
4. Treat ROI and yield as profitability measures, not accuracy measures.

### Cited Findings
- RPS was proposed for football because it respects the ordinal H/D/A scale ([Constantinou & Fenton 2012, JQAS 8(1)](https://doi.org/10.1515/1559-0410.1418)). Wheatcroft disputes this: distance sensitivity "adds nothing in terms of the usual aims of using scoring rules", and simulations favour the local Ignorance (log) score, which the paper "strongly recommends" ([Wheatcroft 2021, JQAS 17(4):273–287](https://doi.org/10.1515/jqas-2019-0089); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08980)). The project's primary metric is already log-loss, which is consistent. The docstring in `backtest/metrics.py` ("Dla 1X2 RPS jest metryka wlasciwa", i.e. RPS is the proper metric for 1X2) and note 12 should be updated.
- Log, Brier and RPS are all strictly proper scoring rules. The log score is the local one ([Gneiting & Raftery 2007, JASA 102(477):359–378](https://doi.org/10.1198/016214506000001437)).
- Koopman & Lit evaluated with RPS and "the Diebold and Mariano (1995) statistic to test for equal predictive accuracy" ([Koopman & Lit 2019 PDF](https://research.vu.nl/ws/portalfiles/portal/151597369/Forecasting_football_match_results_in_national_league_competitions_using_scoredriven_time_series_models.pdf); [Diebold & Mariano 1995, JBES 13(3):253–263](https://doi.org/10.1080/07350015.1995.10524599)). The repo's clustered bootstrap of mean log-loss differences is an equivalent paired approach.
- On calibration diagnostics, the classical binning approach "has been hampered by a lack of stability under unavoidable, ad hoc implementation decisions". CORP (isotonic/PAV-based) gives consistent, optimally binned reliability diagrams and a "CORP-based Brier-score decomposition that generalizes to any proper scoring rule" ([Dimitriadis, Gneiting & Jordan 2021, PNAS 118(8)](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2016191118)). The classic reliability, resolution and uncertainty decomposition: [Murphy 1973, J. Appl. Meteorology 12(4):595–600](https://doi.org/10.1175/1520-0450(1973)012<0595:ANVPOT>2.0.CO;2). The repo's ECE (`backtest/metrics.py:83`) uses 20 fixed bins and pools the H, D and A probabilities into one set of pairs.
- Betting returns "should not be treated as a valid measure of model accuracy", because positive returns can arise "systematically or randomly … in the absence of a superior model accuracy" ([Wunderlich & Memmert 2020, IJF 36(2):713–722](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijforecast.2019.08.009), summary via [DSHS](https://fis.dshs-koeln.de/en/publications/are-betting-returns-a-useful-measure-of-accuracy-in-sports-foreca/)).
- For betting decisions, choosing models by calibration gave +34.69% ROI against −35.17% for choosing by accuracy (NBA data). A corrigendum was issued, but the authors state the conclusion holds ([Walsh & Joshi 2024, MLwA 16:100539](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.mlwa.2024.100539); [arXiv v4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06021); [corrigendum](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666827025000106)). Note 12's "accuracy jako metryka pomocnicza" (accuracy as an auxiliary metric) is consistent with this.
- Time-ordered, out-of-sample evaluation is recommended for non-stationary series ([Cerqueira et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10994-020-05910-7)). The nested walk-forward with season folds (`app/config/nested_goals_plan.example.json`) conforms.
- Even in fully efficient markets, significant single-season effects are "likely to be observed" ([Winkelmann et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1177/15270025231204997)). This supports the notes' multi-window stability requirement.
- The bootstrap clusters by calendar date of kickoff (`app/src/model_buk/evaluation/archive_promotion.py:125`), not by matchday or round. This captures same-day dependence but not dependence across a round spread over several days, or serial dependence across weeks. (This description comes from the code; the task brief says "matchday".)

### Inferences
- The project's diagnosis, "model knows too little" rather than "overconfident", is exactly what a CORP or Murphy decomposition would test formally: low discrimination/resolution with low miscalibration. I recommend reporting MCB, DSC and UNC components for the model and the market per league. This would turn the HANDOFF claim into a measured one.
- Multiple testing: with 22 leagues each tested at a one-sided 97.5% lower bound, the chance that at least one league shows a "positive lower CI" when no real edge exists is about 1 − 0.975²² ≈ 0.43, assuming independence (my calculation). The single league P1 with a positive lower bound against opening (MODEL-BUK-1-STAGE-RESULTS.md, form ablation) is therefore not evidence of an edge. A Holm or Benjamini–Hochberg adjustment should be part of the promotion gate.
- Benchmarks in note 12 should add a results-only Elo/pi-rating model and a Shin-de-vigged market, because Shin probabilities were more accurate than basic normalisation for every bookmaker/sport pair in [Štrumbelj 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijforecast.2014.02.008).
- CLV is used in the project as a market metric. I found no peer-reviewed validation of CLV as a predictor of long-run profit in this search, so it should stay a practitioner heuristic and be treated with the same caution as ROI.

### Gaps
- I did not find peer-reviewed guidance specific to cluster definitions (date vs matchday vs week) for football forecast comparison.
- I did not find a peer-reviewed study validating CLV.

## Q6. Are the leakage rules in note 11 complete by current standards?

### Takeaway
Note 11 is correct as far as it goes. The four timestamps (`event_time`, `published_at`, `ingested_at`, `known_at`), `known_at ≤ prediction_time`, and the listed classic leaks (lineups at T−24h, realised weather, closing odds used early, rolling means that include the current match, random splits, full-sample normalisation, current player names) match the standard taxonomies. It misses several categories that modern checklists include: selection or tuning on the test data, duplicates, non-independence, sampling or survivorship bias, retroactive data revisions (xG models, corrections), odds-timestamp semantics, rescheduled matches, and de-vig or calibrator choice made on the full sample.

### Cited Findings
- Leakage formalisation and avoidance: [Kaufman, Rosset, Perlich & Stitelman 2012, ACM TKDD 6(4):1–21](https://doi.org/10.1145/2382577.2382579).
- The eight-type taxonomy: L1 train/test separation (no test set, pre-processing on train+test, feature selection on train+test, duplicates); L2 illegitimate features; L3 test distribution (temporal leakage, non-independence, sampling bias). Leakage affected at least 294 papers across 17 fields ([Kapoor & Narayanan 2023, Patterns 4(9):100804](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patter.2023.100804); [project page](https://reproducible.cs.princeton.edu/)). Reporting checklist: [REFORMS, arXiv 2308.07832](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.07832).
- Mapping of note 11 to that taxonomy:
  - Covered: temporal leakage (rolling means, closing odds, lineups, weather), pre-processing on the full data (normalisation and imputation), and illegitimate features (post-match corrections, current names).
  - **Not covered:**
    - (a) Feature, model or hyperparameter selection using test data (L1.3). The project's code already guards this with nested folds and flags the post-hoc 50/50 blend (MODEL-BUK-1-STAGE-RESULTS.md), but note 11 does not list it.
    - (b) Duplicates across sources (L1.4), such as the same match from football-data and API-Football under different IDs.
    - (c) Non-independence (L3.2): same-round matches, and paired train/test matches in cup ties.
    - (d) Sampling bias (L3.3): leagues or seasons included because data became available later, and survivorship of teams.
- xG values depend on the provider's model and data availability ([Robberechts & Davis 2020](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-64912-8_2)). If a provider later re-computes xG with a newer model, historical xG is not point-in-time. **This is an inference**: I found no primary source documenting retroactive xG revisions by specific providers.
- Odds-timestamp semantics: the project itself discovered that football-data.co.uk "opening" columns are a Friday snapshot (`HANDOFF.md`; `docs/handoff-archiwum/HANDOFF-2026-09-09-po-backtescie.md:57,105`), and the replay is marked `promotion_eligible=false` because quote timestamps are missing (MODEL-BUK-1-STAGE-RESULTS.md §2). Note 11's generic "closing odds used earlier" rule does not cover **mislabelled snapshot times**.
- The 2020 Bundesliga case, where bookmakers "had problems to adjust" to vanished home advantage ([Deutscher, Winkelmann & Ötting 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.05417)), shows that regime breaks create apparent edges. Backtests that span such breaks need regime-aware splits.

### Inferences
Rules to add to note 11 (each maps to a Kapoor & Narayanan category or to the project's own findings):
1. **Selection leakage:** any choice of model, hyperparameter, de-vig method, calibrator, blend weight or feature set is made on inner folds only. The de-vig method must not be chosen by its calibration on the whole archive.
2. **Duplicate and entity resolution:** a cross-provider match key with deduplication before splitting, plus handling of team renames and mergers.
3. **Rescheduled or postponed matches and time zones:** use the originally scheduled kickoff for the "available at" logic of odds and news, and compute `rest_days` from actual kickoffs known at prediction time.
4. **Data revisions:** version provider models (xG) and store the first-published value. Awarded or annulled results (for example 3:0 walkovers) are flagged and excluded from training targets.
5. **Snapshot semantics for odds:** each odds column carries a verified capture time. "Opening" means the first observed quote, not a provider label.
6. **Sampling and survivorship:** freeze the league and season universe ex ante, and report which leagues were added after seeing data (note 19's quality gate must not use future information).
7. **Third-party ratings** (for example ClubElo or SPI) may be retro-computed or market-informed. The repo's `backtest/elo.py` docstring already states this principle, but note 11 should list it explicitly.
8. **Regime breaks:** COVID/no-fans, VAR and rule changes should be tested as separate evaluation strata.

### Gaps
- I found no football-specific, peer-reviewed leakage checklist. The recommendations above adapt general ML-leakage standards (Kaufman et al. 2012; Kapoor & Narayanan 2023) to football.
- I did not verify football-data.co.uk's own documentation of odds collection times in this session (the site returned HTTP 429). The Friday-snapshot semantics rest on the project's internal HANDOFF notes.
